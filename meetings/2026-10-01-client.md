## Discussion notes

### Purpose and audiences
Julie and Dr. Rachel Chung are preparing an introductory chapter for a book associated with a Pension Research Council conference on AI and retirement. The index would provide context and statistics, with intended credit to the student team and a reference to the resource if used. Publication is not guaranteed.

Julie confirmed two perspectives: professionals asking what applications their firms could use, and students asking what skills they need and where they fit. She floated splitting these perspectives between the two teams; no assignment was agreed.

### Firm universe and pilot
Julie suggested starting with 4–5 major publicly traded banks, naming JPMorgan as an example. The shared material also showed Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Citigroup, and U.S. Bank as examples, not a selected sample. Industry classification documentation may help define the universe.

Subsequent comparisons could include retirement recordkeepers, asset managers, and wealth management / broker-dealers. Firms may operate in multiple subsectors; inclusion should be transparent. Final firms, geography, coverage period, and classification rules remain open.

### Measurement
Julie wants more detail than word counts alone because equally frequent AI mentions can describe very different uses. Options included a word-count baseline with a supplementary evidence measure, percentages of firms using AI for a purpose, and possibly firm-size-weighted percentages. No scoring formula, weighting, taxonomy, or normalization was finalized. Within-subsector normalization was asked about but not resolved.

### Sources and references
- SEC EDGAR 10-Ks and 10-Qs; earnings-call transcripts from company investor-relations pages; careers pages and job postings; press releases and news.
- LinkedIn and public forums were exploratory suggestions. Julie noted uneven employee-profile coverage and company disclosure.
- Handshake access and permitted collection are unresolved. Bloomberg access at CMU was suggested but not verified; consistency with the public-source requirement needs clarification.
- Restricted university career-site data should not be used. Proprietary individual CFO survey data are unavailable.
- Repository received: https://github.com/FengDong-Elon/ai-intensity-dataset. Its displayed description concerns firm-year AI engagement from 10-K text. Julie relayed a colleague's account of an approximately 500-term AI dictionary; this is secondhand and must be verified against the paper.
- Julie offered a John Graham (Duke) CFO-survey paper, classification documentation, and example transcript links. Receipt of these materials is not confirmed.

### Dashboard and maintenance
Julie envisions an information resource with an overall index for covered institutions, subsector filters, explicit firm membership, and links to relevant research and other indices. She would welcome quarterly automated retrieval and recalculation, if feasible. Changes in quarterly AI language and word clouds were additional ideas, not required features.

Snehal proposed a proof-of-concept checkpoint to refine scope. Explicit acceptance and scheduling were not captured. Hosting, operating costs, and post-course maintenance ownership remain open.
